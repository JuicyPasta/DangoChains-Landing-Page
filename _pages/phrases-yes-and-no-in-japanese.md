<h1>How to say yes and no in Japanese</h1>
<p>"Yes" is <span lang="ja">はい</span> (<i>hai</i>), and it also means "I'm listening" or "here!" when a name is called. Friends say <span lang="ja">うん</span> (<i>un</i>). "No" is <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> (<i>iie</i>) in textbooks, but people rarely say a flat no: they use <span lang="ja">いや</span>, <span lang="ja">違います</span> ("that's not right") or a soft <span lang="ja">ちょっと…</span> ("that's a bit…") and let the sentence trail off.</p>

<h2>The negative-question trap</h2>
<p>Japanese yes and no answer "is what you said right?". Asked <span lang="ja">行かないの？</span> ("You're not going?"), <span lang="ja">はい</span> means "right, I'm not going", where English would say "no".</p>
