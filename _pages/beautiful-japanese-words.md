<h1>Beautiful Japanese words with deep meanings</h1>
<p>Some Japanese words pack a whole scene or feeling into a few kanji, which is why they turn up on lists of "untranslatable" words. Here are 24, with the reading and what each one means in use. A few (<i>wabi-sabi</i>, <i>ikigai</i>) mean something broader in English than in everyday Japanese; the notes say so.</p>

<h2>Feelings and ideas</h2>
<table class="st-table"><thead><tr><th>Word</th><th>Reading</th><th>Romaji</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td lang="ja">物の哀れ</td><td lang="ja">もののあわれ</td><td>mono no aware</td><td>a gentle sadness at how everything passes</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">侘び寂び</td><td lang="ja">わびさび</td><td>wabi-sabi</td><td>quiet, simple beauty that shows age and imperfection; in Japan mostly an art and tea-ceremony term</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">幽玄</td><td lang="ja">ゆうげん</td><td>yūgen</td><td>deep, mysterious beauty that is felt more than seen</td></tr>

<tr><td lang="ja">生き甲斐</td><td lang="ja">いきがい</td><td>ikigai</td><td>what makes life worth living; in Japanese an everyday word, not a self-help system</td></tr>

<tr><td lang="ja">恋の予感</td><td lang="ja">こいのよかん</td><td>koi no yokan</td><td>the feeling, on meeting someone, that you will fall in love</td></tr>
