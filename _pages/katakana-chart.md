<h1>Katakana chart</h1>
<p>Katakana spells the same sounds as <a href="/hiragana-chart/">hiragana</a>, with sharper shapes. It writes words borrowed from other languages (<span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> <i>kōhī</i>, coffee; <span lang="ja">テレビ</span> <i>terebi</i>, TV), foreign names, many animal and plant names in science, sound effects, and words written for emphasis, the way English uses italics. Read the chart by rows: consonant on the left plus vowel at the top.</p>
<p><b>Hear them:</b> tap any character in the charts below (it uses your device's Japanese voice). <b>Test yourself:</b> <a href="/katakana-quiz/">katakana quiz</a> (free, 20 questions a round, with sound).</p>

<h2>The long-vowel mark ー</h2>
<p>Katakana marks a long vowel with a dash, <span lang="ja">ー</span>, instead of a second vowel: <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> <i>kōhī</i> (coffee), <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> <i>kēki</i> (cake), <span lang="ja">スーパー</span> <i>sūpā</i> (supermarket). In vertical text the dash runs top to bottom. A small <span lang="ja">ッ</span> doubles the next consonant, as in hiragana: <span lang="ja">ベッド</span> <i>beddo</i> (bed).</p>
<h2>Extra sounds for foreign words</h2>

<h2>Characters that look alike</h2>
<table class="st-table"><thead><tr><th>Pair</th><th>Romaji</th><th>Tell them apart</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td lang="ja">シ / ツ</td><td>shi / tsu</td><td>シ's dots sit on the left and its stroke sweeps up from the bottom; ツ's dots sit on top and its stroke falls from the top</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">ソ / ン</td><td>so / n</td><td>same rule: ソ's long stroke falls from the top, ン's rises from the bottom</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">ク / ケ / タ</td><td>ku / ke / ta</td><td>ケ has a separate crossbar; タ has an inner stroke</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">ウ / ワ / フ</td><td>u / wa / fu</td><td>count the strokes: 3, 2, 1</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">ノ / メ</td><td>no / me</td><td>メ adds a crossing stroke</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">ロ / コ</td><td>ro / ko</td><td>ロ is closed</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">チ / テ</td><td>chi / te</td><td>チ has a stroke through the middle</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">カ / 力</td><td>ka / chikara (kanji for power)</td><td>the same shape; context tells you which</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
