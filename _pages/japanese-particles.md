<h1>Japanese particles</h1>
<p>Particles are the short words after a noun (or at the end of a sentence) that show what it does: topic, subject, object, place, time. English uses word order and prepositions for this; Japanese uses particles, so word order is freer. Three are spelled one way and read another: <span lang="ja">は</span> is read <i>wa</i>, <span lang="ja">へ</span> <i>e</i>, and <span lang="ja">を</span> <i>o</i>.</p>

<h2>は or が?</h2>
<p><span lang="ja">は</span> sets the topic, what the sentence is about, and the new information comes after it: <span lang="ja">私は田中です</span> ("As for me, I'm Tanaka"). <span lang="ja">が</span> marks the subject when it is itself the new information: asked <span lang="ja">誰が田中さんですか</span> ("Who is Tanaka?"), you answer <span lang="ja">私が田中です</span> ("I am Tanaka"). Question words (<span lang="ja">誰</span>, <span lang="ja">何</span>) take <span lang="ja">が</span>, never <span lang="ja">は</span>.</p>
<h2>に or で?</h2>
<p>Both can mean "at" or "in". <span lang="ja">に</span> is where something <i>is</i> or <i>goes</i>: <span lang="ja">公園に猫がいます</span> (there's a cat in the park). <span lang="ja">で</span> is where something <i>happens</i>: <span lang="ja">公園で遊びます</span> (I play in the park).</p>
