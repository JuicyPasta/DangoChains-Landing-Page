<h1>How to say "how are you" in Japanese</h1>
<p>The textbook phrase is <span lang="ja">お元気ですか</span> (<i>o-genki desu ka</i>), literally "are you well?". Unlike English "how are you", it isn't a daily greeting: people ask it when they haven't seen someone for a while, or in letters. With people you see every day, a plain <span lang="ja">おはよう</span> or <span lang="ja">こんにちは</span> does the job.</p>
<table class="st-table"><thead><tr><th>Japanese</th><th>Romaji</th><th>Politeness</th><th>When to use it</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td lang="ja">お元気ですか</td><td>o-genki desu ka</td><td>polite</td><td>after some time apart; letters and emails</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">元気？</td><td>genki?</td><td>casual</td><td>to friends</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">元気だった？</td><td>genki datta?</td><td>casual</td><td>"have you been well?" when you meet again</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">最近どう？</td><td>saikin dō?</td><td>casual</td><td>how have you been lately?</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">調子はどう？</td><td>chōshi wa dō?</td><td>casual</td><td>how's it going?</td></tr>
<tr><td lang="ja">大丈夫？</td><td>daijōbu?</td><td>casual</td><td>are you OK? (when something seems wrong)</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
