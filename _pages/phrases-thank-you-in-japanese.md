<h1>How to say thank you in Japanese</h1>
<p>The everyday word is <span lang="ja">ありがとう</span> (<i>arigatō</i>). Add <span lang="ja">ございます</span> to make it polite: that is the safe choice with strangers, shop staff, teachers and anyone older than you. For something that is already finished, such as a meal at a restaurant or a lesson, people switch to the past form <span lang="ja">ありがとうございました</span>. And when someone went to trouble for you, Japanese often says <span lang="ja">すみません</span> ("sorry for the bother") where English says thanks.</p>

<h2>How to answer a thank you</h2>

<h2>Thanks you can only say at the table</h2>
<p>Before eating, people say <span lang="ja">いただきます</span> (<i>itadakimasu</i>); after eating, <span lang="ja">ごちそうさまでした</span> (<i>gochisōsama deshita</i>), which thanks whoever cooked or paid. Both are also said to staff when leaving a small restaurant.</p>
<p><small>Where it comes from: <span lang="ja">ありがとう</span> was once written <span lang="ja">有難う</span>, from <span lang="ja">有り難い</span> (<i>arigatai</i>, "hard to have", so rare and precious). Today it is almost always written in hiragana.</small></p>
