<h1>Hiragana chart</h1>
<p>Hiragana is the first Japanese script to learn: 46 basic characters, each one a syllable. It writes grammar (endings and particles like <span lang="ja">は</span>, <span lang="ja">を</span>, <span lang="ja">です</span>), native words without common kanji, and the furigana printed over kanji. Read the chart by rows: the consonant on the left plus the vowel at the top. Japanese order runs あいうえお, not a e i o u.</p>
<p><b>Hear them:</b> tap any character in the charts below (it uses your device's Japanese voice). <b>Test yourself:</b> <a href="/hiragana-quiz/">hiragana quiz</a> (free, 20 questions a round, with sound).</p>

<p><b>Watch these:</b> <span lang="ja">し</span> is <i>shi</i>, <span lang="ja">ち</span> <i>chi</i>, <span lang="ja">つ</span> <i>tsu</i> and <span lang="ja">ふ</span> <i>fu</i> (between f and h). <span lang="ja">を</span> sounds like <i>o</i> and is only used as a particle. As particles, <span lang="ja">は</span> is read <i>wa</i> and <span lang="ja">へ</span> <i>e</i>. <span lang="ja">ん</span> is the only consonant that stands alone.</p>
<h2>Dakuten and handakuten: voiced sounds</h2>
<p>Two small marks (<span lang="ja">゛</span>, <i>dakuten</i>) make a sound voiced: k → g, s → z, t → d, h → b. A small circle (<span lang="ja">゜</span>, <i>handakuten</i>) turns h into p. <span lang="ja">ぢ</span> and <span lang="ja">づ</span> sound like <span lang="ja">じ</span> and <span lang="ja">ず</span> and are rare.</p>

<h2>Combinations (yōon)</h2>
<p>A small <span lang="ja">ゃ</span>, <span lang="ja">ゅ</span> or <span lang="ja">ょ</span> after an i-column character makes one syllable: <span lang="ja">き</span> + <span lang="ja">ゃ</span> = <span lang="ja">きゃ</span> <i>kya</i>. Compare <span lang="ja">びょういん</span> (<i>byōin</i>, hospital) with <span lang="ja">びよういん</span> (<i>biyōin</i>, hair salon).</p>
