<h1>Japanese last names</h1>
<p>Japan has well over 100,000 surnames, but a few dozen cover a large share of the population. Most describe a place: a rice field (<span lang="ja">田</span>), a mountain (<span lang="ja">山</span>), a river, a bridge, a village. Many with <span lang="ja">藤</span> (wisteria) trace back to the old Fujiwara clan. Here are the 30 most common, roughly in order of frequency (rankings differ a little by source), with what their kanji mean.</p>

<h2>How names work</h2>
<p>In Japanese the family name comes first: <span lang="ja">田中 太郎</span> is Tarō Tanaka. People are addressed by family name plus <span lang="ja">さん</span> (<i>Tanaka-san</i>); given names are for family and close friends. The same kanji can be read differently in different families (<span lang="ja">中島</span> Nakajima or Nakashima), so people often confirm the reading.</p>
